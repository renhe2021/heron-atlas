Philip R. Lane: The AI opportunity for Europe

Published Oct 14, 2026 02:30 Beijing

First read

Artificial intelligence is a general-purpose technology capable of raising global living standards and driving productivity gains, but the realization of its benefits depends on coordinated efforts among multiple participants.

The ECB is closely monitoring the impact of artificial intelligence on the euro-area economy, noting that firms plan to allocate about 10% of total investment to artificial intelligence this year, and that AI-related loans account for roughly one quarter of recent corporate credit growth.

Surveys show that AI adoption in Europe is rising rapidly, with 52% of employees reporting using artificial intelligence at work, but no substantial impact on aggregate productivity has yet been observed.

There is a clear gap between the pace of digital investment growth in Europe and that in the United States: US digital investment growth over the past two years was twice that of the euro area, and the United States holds 75% of global AI computing power while Europe accounts for only 5%; the global AI investment boom has pushed up long-term interest rates, creating a spillover effect of tighter financial conditions for the euro-area bond market.

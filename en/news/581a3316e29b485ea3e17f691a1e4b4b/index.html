Frank Elderson: Interview with Die Presse

Published Oct 20, 2026 15:00 Beijing

First read

ECB Executive Board member Elderson said that the cost of regulation is far lower than the cost of the 2008 financial crisis, and that simplifying regulation is not the same as deregulation; resilience is the foundation of sustainable growth and competitiveness.

He pointed out that the biggest obstacle to the competitiveness of European banking is market fragmentation, and called for completing the single market, the savings and investment union, and the banking union including a European deposit insurance scheme.

He advocated raising the asset threshold for small, non-complex banks from €5 billion to €10 billion, so that about 150 banks would be subject to a simplified reporting regime.

He warned that artificial intelligence brings hidden risks such as cyber risk, model bias, data quality and concentration among cloud service providers, and required banks to submit by the end of October a concrete action plan for addressing AI-related cyber risk.

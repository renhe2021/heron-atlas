Philip R. Lane: Interview with Ansa

Published Oct 6, 2026 15:00 Beijing2026-10-06 07:00 UTC

First read

ECB Executive Board member Lane said the Middle East conflict has pushed up energy prices, but the strength of pass-through to broader inflation and the economy remains uncertain, and no strong second-round effects have yet been seen.

He noted that the significant rise in global long-term yields stems partly from financing demand brought by the US AI investment boom, and the ECB will take overall financial conditions, including long-term interest rates, into account in its monetary policy assessment.

Lane believes the main driver of recent rate hikes is the inflation impact of the energy shock, and warned that broad-based fiscal expansion is not conducive to inflation returning to the 2% target in a timely manner, and support for low-income groups should be as targeted as possible.

He mentioned that Italy's September inflation reading was 4.1%, and expects Italian wages to outpace inflation in 2027 and 2028, while emphasizing that the government, when formulating its budget under a high interest rate environment, needs to take a long-term view and effectively push forward growth-enhancing reforms.

InterpretationLane emphasized that rising long-term yields themselves would restrain the euro area economy and lower inflation, implying that if externally driven financing conditions continue to tighten, the ECB may not need to raise the policy rate further and may even have room to cut; at the same time, his warning about broad fiscal expansion means that tension between monetary policy and fiscal policy persists, which could affect the euro exchange rate and euro area sovereign bond spreads.

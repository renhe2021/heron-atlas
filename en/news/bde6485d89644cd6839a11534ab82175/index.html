Financial System Report (October 2026)

Published Oct 23, 2026 14:00 Beijing2026-10-23 06:00 UTC

First read

The Bank of Japan released its October 2026 Financial System Report, judging that Japan's financial system remains stable overall against a backdrop in which expanding global AI-related demand has improved market sentiment, but uncertainty remains high regarding the Middle East situation and the overseas non-bank financial intermediation (NBFI) sector.

The report noted that stock price indicators continue to deviate upward from their trend line, real estate prices are rising overall with the pace of increase accelerating in the three major metropolitan areas, and overseas NBFI is playing an increasingly prominent role in financing AI-related investment; if AI profit expectations decline, the shock could be amplified through their portfolio adjustments and spill over to Japan.

Macro stress test results showed that Japanese banks have sufficient capital bases and stable funding sources and can withstand compound stress including a shock on the scale of the global financial crisis as well as the simultaneous occurrence of geopolitical risk, a decline in AI expectations and a sharp rise in interest rates.

The report also assessed the impact of rising raw material and energy prices on small and medium-sized enterprises and the impact of rising interest rates on the corporate and household sectors, and noted that advances in "frontier AI" have further intensified cyber security threats, requiring enhanced resilience of financial institutions and the financial system as a whole.

InterpretationThe report confirms that Japan's financial system is overall sound in a rising interest rate environment, but at the same time warns that increased valuation losses on yen bonds, declining deposit stickiness, and a retreat from AI investment may transmit risks via overseas NBFIs, implying that the Bank of Japan will continue to closely monitor financial market stability during the rate-hiking cycle, which is of reference significance for JGB yield curve and yen exchange rate expectations.

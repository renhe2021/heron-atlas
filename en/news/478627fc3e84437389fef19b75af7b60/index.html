Philip R. Lane: Euro liquidity lines and the international monetary system

Published Oct 21, 2026 12:15 Beijing

First read

ECB Executive Board member Philip R. Lane set out the need to supplement the domestic liquidity framework through international liquidity lines such as repos and swaps, in order to mitigate adverse spillovers from a surge in demand for euro liquidity abroad or disruptions to cross-border flows.

This year the ECB decided to enhance the Eurosystem repo facility for central banks (EUREP), opening standing access in principle to all central banks, with a maximum of €50 billion per transaction, priced at the main refinancing operations rate plus a spread set by the Governing Council.

Swap lines complement EUREP, can accept local-currency collateral and operate more directly through the bank lending channel, but because of higher risk are limited to partner central banks with high-rated sovereign backing.

International liquidity lines help enhance the international role of the euro, expand the scale of euro-denominated debt issuance and reduce the sensitivity of euro-area import prices to external shocks, thereby strengthening monetary policy transmission.
